### 2. Theoretical Background & Governing Equations

#### 2.1 Numerical Error Norms
Let $\mathbf{x}_n^{\text{sim}}$ denote the computed particle position at time $t_n = n \Delta t$, and $\mathbf{x}_{\text{exact}}(t_n)$ the exact continuous solution. Over a total duration $T$ consisting of $N = T / \Delta t$ discrete steps, the discrete error norms are:

1. **Max-Norm ($L_\infty$):**
   $$L_\infty = \max_{1 \le n \le N} \|\mathbf{x}_n^{\text{sim}} - \mathbf{x}_{\text{exact}}(t_n)\|$$
2. **Mean-Square Norm ($L_2$):**
   $$L_2 = \sqrt{\frac{1}{N} \sum_{n=1}^N \|\mathbf{x}_n^{\text{sim}} - \mathbf{x}_{\text{exact}}(t_n)\|^2}$$

#### 2.2 Asymptotic Order of Convergence
For an integration algorithm of global order $p$:
$$\text{Error}(\Delta t) \approx C\,(\Delta t)^p \quad \text{as } \Delta t \to 0$$

Taking the natural logarithm:
$$\ln(\text{Error}) \approx \ln(C) + p \ln(\Delta t)$$
On a $\ln(\text{Error})$ vs $\ln(\Delta t)$ plot, the slope of the curve corresponds to the convergence order $p$.

#### 2.3 Empirical Order of Convergence (EOC)
When halving the timestep ($\Delta t_2 = \Delta t_1 / 2$), the reduction ratio $R$ is:
$$R = \frac{\text{Error}(\Delta t_1)}{\text{Error}(\Delta t_2)} \approx 2^p$$

The empirical order $p$ between two refinement levels is:
$$p = \frac{\ln(E_1 / E_2)}{\ln(\Delta t_1 / \Delta t_2)} = \frac{\ln(R)}{\ln(2)}$$
